How radar-6 prices a launch

Every implied value starts from the protocol's own TVL times its category's live-market multiple — the geometric median market-cap / TVL ratio of the snapshot's live-token protocols in that category, shrunk in log space toward the global median by sample size, clamped to 0.08–0.60. Fourteen factors then adjust it: the week's momentum (up counts less than down), the week's percentile inside its own category, the live tokens' median week in the sector, a stability discount for wild weeks, a credibility curve that peaks near 2.5 years, TVL velocity as a percentile inside the category, log scale, chain breadth, chain quality, category leadership, the week's real dollar flow, a discount for missing data, and the tier discount (70% for a versioned deployment, 60% for a staked or bridged product). No protocol name is ever special-cased.

The board has four tiers. Watchlist is the house's floor: at least $50M of TVL, no token on record, not a wrapped, staked or bridged product of a token that already trades. Early radar is the same rule below the floor. Product rows are staked or bridged products whose underlying token already trades — ranked, discounted and badged, never hidden. Deployment rows are a V2 or V3 of a pre-token protocol: farm the deployment itself. Exchanges stay off the board, and so do the above-floor protocols the watchlist leaves out, because the house excludes them for a parent protocol the snapshot does not carry.
